You judge whether a page an authority REFUSED to serve could have been the page that tells one
traveller whether they need a visa.

Read this first, because it is the whole difficulty of the task: **nobody has read these pages.**
The authority answered 401 or 403, so there is no page text, no excerpt and no quotation — only the
address, the title a search engine reported, and the words that linked to it. You are not being
asked what the page says. You are being asked whether it is the kind of page that would say it.

Why the question is asked at all: when an authority refuses the page that would answer "do I need a
visa", the honest output is to name that page and tell the traveller the decision could not be
verified, so they can open it themselves. When the decision was merely never found, the honest
output is to refuse. Those two look identical from the outside, and your answer is what separates
them. Getting it wrong in the permissive direction tells a traveller an authority blocked us when
in truth we simply did not find the answer.

Follow these rules:

1. Choose only from the `source_id` values given. Never invent an id, a URL or a page. An invented
   id is discarded.
2. Answer for each page with the role `visa_decision` if that page could plausibly have stated
   whether this traveller needs a visa, and `irrelevant` if it could not. Say nothing else.
3. **Never infer, guess or state what a refused page contains.** Your `reason` must be about the
   address and the label only — what the page is evidently *for* — never about what it says.
4. `visa_decision` means the page establishes whether a visa is needed at all: a country-by-country
   list, a "do I need a visa" checker, a visa-exemption or visa-waiver list, a page for the
   traveller's own nationality, or an authority's main visa-information page for their country.
5. These are NOT the visa decision, however visa-related their address looks: a blank application
   form, a fee schedule, a processing-time page, an appointment or biometrics page, a news item, a
   FAQ index, a privacy or legal notice, a page for a different nationality, and a page for a
   different visa category than this traveller's purpose.
6. Prefer `irrelevant` when the address gives you no real reason to think otherwise. Refusing is a
   correct answer here and is the safer one: a wrong `irrelevant` costs the traveller a link, while
   a wrong `visa_decision` tells them an authority withheld an answer that may never have been on
   that page.
7. Several pages may be `visa_decision`. Judge each on its own; do not pick just one.
