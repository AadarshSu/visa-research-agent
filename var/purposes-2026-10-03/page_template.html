After your decisions

Fixed · decision 1

Japan transit no longer says "visa required"

The short-stay exemption list no longer decides transit, in the role judge or the plan. No page about transit through Japan was found, so the corridor now refuses rather than giving the wrong answer. Vietnam transit, which had made the same move, refuses for the same reason.

Working · decision 4

Eleven answers now carry their condition

China transit says no visa only if you stay in the restricted area for under 24 hours; South Korea the same; Germany only if you stay airside and fly on outside Schengen; Turkey's e-ATV only if you connect to Mexico, Panama, Colombia or Venezuela; Spain and France for a US student only for more than 90 days; Japan study only if you hold a Certificate of Eligibility. The condition is shown directly under the decision on the plan page, and a conditional plan is never verified.

To grade · wording

Three conditions are worded worse than they should be

New Zealand transit strings four facts into one clause. Spain transit says "you do not meet one of the stated airport-transit-visa exemptions" without naming them. South Africa transit adds "and are not a deportee", which every ordinary traveller meets. The prompt asks for only the deciding fact; these did not keep to it.

Working · decision 2

Thailand study resolves instead of refusing, and keeps its checklist

The role judge set aside the tourism-only exemption and credited the Ministry's page naming the ED visa for study. The plan still leaves the decision open because no page says Indians must hold it, which is the case-by-case you allowed. South Africa study left the decision open once and stated it once across two runs: the visitor exemption list now says nothing about study, so the answer rests on whether a study-visa page is read.

Mine · fixed before measuring

A first draft of the conditional rule was too strict

It required a source to state both sides of the condition. China and Germany transit, which had answered "no visa", went open, and Japan study refused. It now states the side a source states, provided none contradicts it. Japan from the UK and Singapore for a Philippine passport were run three times each after both drafts: unchanged, verified, no condition.

Findings from the first round

Probably wrong · owner's decision

Japan transit says "visa required" from the short-stay exemption list

The plan rests on India's absence from Japan's short-term visa-exemption list. That list governs entering Japan, not changing planes, and rule 8f requires the list to be this trip's. The embassy fee page it also read says Transit Visas have not been issued since 1 July 2026, and the plan's first open question asks which route applies at all. Status is partial, but the headline still says a visa is required.

Product gap · transit

Transit answers turn on three facts the form never asks

Layover length, whether the traveller leaves the airport, and the onward country. The judge found the deciding page in most transit corridors: Korea (visa-free under 24 hours airside), South Africa (no transit visa at four airports), Spain (India is on its airport-transit-visa list), Turkey (an e-ATV only for connections to Mexico, Panama, Colombia or Venezuela). With only yes, no or unverified available, each correct conditional answer became an open decision. Turkey's became a full refusal, discarding a complete answer.

Infrastructure · not purpose

The stores are past the 168-hour stale ceiling

The stores were rebuilt 24–26 September. Today a page that needs a browser and misses the corridor's five renders, or whose host's robots.txt fails, can no longer fall back to its stored copy. This refused Korea business (the Chennai exemption table) and Vietnam in every purpose, tourism included (the Ministry's exemption table; its robots.txt answered 502). The refusals are correct under the rules; a weekly refresh (TODO item 20) is what prevents them.

Owner's decision · study

Thailand study: the answer was on two pages and neither was taken alone

Thailand's 2026 notices say the exemption and visa-on-arrival schemes cover tourism only and do not affect other visa categories; the e-Visa Non-Immigrant ED page names the student visa and its documents. Together they say a study trip needs the ED visa. No single page says so for India, so the decision went unfilled and the found checklist was discarded. Whether a tourism-only exemption may settle "visa required" for another purpose would change what a plan may conclude.

Product gap · study

Study needs a length of stay

A two-week course and a degree have different answers. The plans handled the missing field well: Spain, Japan, Switzerland, France, Germany and Spain for US passports each asked how long the course is rather than assuming. Switzerland and Japan found the long-stay student checklist. The traveller profile has no field to answer with.

Safe · EU tier

The EU's short-stay rule did not produce a false "no visa" for study

France, Germany and Spain for a US passport studying all held the decision open and asked the duration. France read France-Visas' own United States page (visa needed beyond 90 days). Spain for an Indian passport cites both the EU regulation and the New Delhi embassy's study-visa page.

To grade

Two visa-free transit answers and one business decision

China transit says no visa (verified) on the National Immigration Administration's 24-hour rule for all nationalities, airside only; the headline does not carry that limit. Germany transit says no visa (verified) on the India embassy's notice that Indians no longer need an airport transit visa. Thailand business says visa required (verified) on the MFA's "foreigners wishing to conduct business must apply for a Non-B"; whether that covers a short meeting is the question.

Caught overclaim

Switzerland study: the plan writer refused what the judge assumed

The judge credited the federal visa list as placing India in Annex I, but the readable text holds only the footnotes, not the country table. The plan writer saw no India entry and left the decision open, which is the correct result.
